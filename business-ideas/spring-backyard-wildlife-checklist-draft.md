## Draft copy

### Spring Backyard Wildlife Checklist
Use this simple checklist to get your yard, garden, or cabin space ready for birds, butterflies, hummingbirds, squirrels, chipmunks, and other little critters over the next 30 days.

---

## 1) Refresh your water sources
Water is one of the fastest ways to make a yard more wildlife-friendly in spring.

- Clean birdbaths and refill with fresh water.
- Add a shallow water spot for small birds, butterflies, and little critters.
- Place water near cover, but not so close that predators can hide right next to it.
- Check water often as temperatures rise.

## 2) Restart feeders the clean way
Winter feeding stations often need a reset.

- Wash bird feeders and let them dry before refilling.
- Throw out old, wet, or moldy seed.
- Refill feeders with fresh food.
- Get hummingbird feeders cleaned and ready before migration picks up.
- If you want to support squirrels or chipmunks too, think about a separate feeding area so everything is not competing in one small spot.

## 3) Create shelter and safe cover
Early spring weather can still be rough, and wildlife needs places to rest and hide.

- Keep some shrubs, brushy edges, or tucked-away cover.
- Avoid over-pruning everything at once.
- Leave a few natural areas that feel safe and calm.
- If you use birdhouses or nest boxes, inspect and clean them now.

## 4) Plant and plan for pollinators
Spring is the time to prepare for hummingbirds, butterflies, bees, and other pollinators.

- Choose flowers that support pollinators and bloom over different parts of the season.
- Favor native plants when you can.
- Plan for early, mid, and late blooms instead of one short burst of flowers.
- Set aside a sunny area for pollinator-friendly planting.
- Think about flowers for nectar, but also plants that provide cover and habitat.

## 5) Don’t over-clean too early
A perfectly “clean” yard is often less helpful for wildlife.

- Clean up gradually instead of stripping everything bare.
- Leave some stems, leaf litter, and natural ground cover in selected areas.
- Remember that many beneficial insects use these spaces to overwinter.
- Tidy with intention instead of removing all the wildness.

## 6) Prepare for spring arrivals
Different wildlife becomes more active as spring settles in.

- Watch for nesting behavior in birds.
- Be ready for hummingbirds as migration reaches your area.
- Notice early butterfly activity on warm days.
- Look for squirrels and chipmunks becoming more active around edges, feeders, and shelter.
- Keep a simple notebook of what shows up and what seems to help.

## 7) Make one improvement each week
Small steady changes beat one giant weekend project.

Week 1:
- Clean and refill water sources.
- Clean feeders.

Week 2:
- Inspect houses, shelters, and cover.
- Refresh one wildlife-friendly area.

Week 3:
- Add flowers or prep a pollinator bed.
- Create or improve a safe resting/cover zone.

Week 4:
- Fine-tune feeder placement, water, and planting.
- Observe what wildlife is using your space.
