    Prevention / The unglamorous useful things

    A calmer routine.
    A closer eye.

    1. Look regularly. Notice change before a small patch becomes a whole-plant problem.
    2. Leave room. Crowded growth makes access and airflow harder.
    3. Keep water steady. Pay attention to plant and soil conditions rather than reacting only after stress appears.
    4. Use a physical barrier where it fits. Match covers or netting to the crop, pest and pollination needs.
    5. Act on an identification. Hand-picking and cleanup can be useful; what you remove should follow what you found.

    Before the panic fix

    Natural doesn’t mean
    automatically right.

    Do not add every companion at once, turn support plants into competitors, or assume a homemade spray is safe because it sounds old-fashioned.

    Trap crops and repellence depend on a particular pest and plant relationship. Generic scented-herb lists are not a treatment plan.

    Read the evidence-led companion-planting context from UMN Extension.
