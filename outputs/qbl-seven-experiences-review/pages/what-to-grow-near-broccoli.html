    What to Grow Near Broccoli

    Broccoli usually does better with a few thoughtful neighbors around it, especially ones that help keep the bed mixed, inspectable, and not overloaded with more bulky brassicas than it can handle. The best companions are often the ones that support airflow, harvest access, and a less one-note pest situation.

    Broccoli companions at a glance

    Best easy companions

    Onions, dill, lettuce, beets, and flowers nearby are some of the simplest sensible companions for broccoli.

    What helps most

    Spacing, airflow, and fewer same-family plants crammed together usually matter more than a long companion chart.

    Good use of the edges

    Use the edges for onions, lettuce, or flowers, not another heavy crop that turns the patch dense and hard to inspect.

    What to avoid

    Do not let the bed become one thick brassica block where worms, aphids, and damp foliage problems are easy to miss.

    Best broccoli support crew

    • Onions or scallions for lower narrow company
    • Dill for a lighter mixed-bed feel
    • Lettuce as a cooler-season front-edge filler
    • Beets for another useful crop without doubling down on brassicas
    • Flowers nearby where insects and pollinators can find them

    Why these broccoli pairings actually make sense

    • Onions and scallions fit because they stay narrow. They give the bed another edible without making the broccoli patch too thick.
    • Dill adds a different structure. It helps the bed feel mixed instead of like one giant leafy brassica patch.
    • Lettuce only works when timing stays in its favor. It makes sense early, but not if it gets buried once broccoli bulks up.
    • Beets are often easier neighbors than another cabbage-family crop. They keep the bed productive without stacking the exact same pest pressures.
    • Flowers help most around the edges. The goal is a livelier bed, not a crowded center that hides damage.

    What usually goes wrong

    • too many brassicas packed together
    • not checking under leaves often enough
    • lettuce or filler crops getting buried and forgotten
    • letting the patch stay damp and crowded

    A simple broccoli bed layout idea

    A beginner-friendly broccoli bed usually works best when broccoli has real elbow room and the companions stay on the outside instead of filling every gap.

    • broccoli spaced as the clear main crop
    • onions or scallions in a lower side row
    • lettuce only as an early cooler-season front edge
    • dill or flowers off to the side, not threaded through the middle
    • space left open for airflow and easy worm checks

    What to be careful about

    Broccoli gets bulky enough that even good companions become bad ones if the patch turns dense and hard to inspect once the leaves fill out.

    • Do not crowd it with more big brassicas.
    • Do not make it hard to check leaf undersides.
    • Do not let the bed stay humid and still.
    • Do not forget that cooler-season timing changes what fits nearby.

    Protect my broccoli, what actually helps?

    If broccoli leaves start looking chewed, ragged, or full of holes, the first pests to suspect are usually cabbage worms, loopers, aphids, or flea beetles. Broccoli almost always does better when these are caught early instead of after the head starts forming.

    • What to watch for Holey leaves, green caterpillars, dark frass, aphids tucked into new growth, and heads that never size up cleanly.
    • What naturally helps most Row cover, hand-picking worms, steady growth, and frequent leaf checks.
    • Check the undersides Caterpillars and eggs often hide there first.
    • Keep the bed moving Good airflow and spacing help broccoli stay less stressed.
    • Do not ignore butterflies White cabbage butterflies are often a warning, not background scenery.
    • Clean up tired brassica leaves Do not let old damaged foliage just sit there.

    Common broccoli troublemakers

    • Cabbage worms Green caterpillars that chew leaves and heads. Quick cure: hand-pick early and inspect leaves often.
    • Cabbage loopers Smooth looping caterpillars that can strip foliage fast. Quick cure: catch them while they are still small.
    • Aphids Cluster in tender growth and around the head. Quick cure: spray them off before they build deep in the plant.
    • Flea beetles Tiny jumpy beetles that pepper leaves with holes. Quick cure: protect young plants early and keep them vigorous.
    • Slugs Can rough up lower leaves in damp beds. Quick cure: reduce hiding spots and inspect early morning or dusk.

    Best first move

    Look under the leaves first, because that is where the real broccoli story usually starts.

    Good natural broccoli pest routine

    • check leaf undersides often
    • hand-pick worms early
    • use row cover when pressure is predictable
    • keep the bed airy and watered
    • remove rough old leaves promptly

    What not to do

    • Do not wait until the leaves or heads are heavily chewed.
    • Do not ignore white cabbage butterflies.
    • Do not let the bed stay crowded and messy.

    Natural remedies people sometimes try

    • Bt (Bacillus thuringiensis) for young cabbage worms and loopers.
    • Neem oil for some early soft-bodied pests, used carefully.
    • Row cover is still one of the most useful low-tech protections here.

    A few more broccoli basics that really matter

    • Soil pH: broccoli often does well around 6.0 to 7.0.
    • Rich soil: broccoli usually appreciates fertile soil.
    • Cool weather: it often performs best outside peak heat.
    • Moisture: steady water helps avoid stress.
    • Airflow: important for cleaner healthier growth.

    Good next reads: Protect My Veggie, Build My Garden Plan, and the crop finder.

    Bottom line

    If you like broccoli, keep the patch mixed but not crowded, and use companions that make pest checks and airflow easier instead of harder.
