    What to Grow Near Cabbage

    Cabbage does best with neighbors that help keep the bed mixed, manageable, and less inviting as one giant brassica buffet. The best companions are usually the ones that stay lower, support the edges, and do not make it harder to spot caterpillars or keep the patch breathing.

    Cabbage companions at a glance

    Best easy companions

    Onions, chives, dill, lettuce, and beets are some of the simplest useful companions for cabbage.

    What helps most

    Airflow, space, and fewer same-family plants packed together usually matter more than a huge companion chart.

    Good use of the edges

    Use the edges for onions, herbs, lettuce, or flowers, not another heavy leafy crop that thickens the patch.

    What to avoid

    Do not let the bed become a crowded block where worm damage, damp foliage, and airflow problems get hidden.

    Best cabbage support crew

    • Onions or chives for narrow nearby company
    • Dill for a lighter mixed-bed feel
    • Lettuce as an early front-edge filler
    • Beets for another useful crop without more brassica pressure
    • Flowers nearby around the outside, not jammed through the center

    Why these cabbage pairings actually make sense

    • Onions and chives fit because they stay narrow. They add usefulness without turning the cabbage row into a leafy traffic jam.
    • Dill helps the bed feel less one-note. A mixed patch is often easier to manage than a solid block of brassicas.
    • Lettuce works best early. It is useful on the front edge before cabbage heads and wrapper leaves start taking over the light.
    • Beets are often easier neighbors than another cabbage-family crop. They keep the bed productive without stacking the same pests in the same space.
    • Flowers belong around the outside. They help more when they add life at the edges instead of crowding the cabbage center.

    What usually goes wrong

    • too many brassicas planted shoulder to shoulder
    • not checking under leaves often enough
    • lettuce or filler crops getting buried and forgotten
    • letting the patch stay damp, dense, and hard to inspect

    A simple cabbage bed layout idea

    A beginner-friendly cabbage bed usually works best when cabbage is clearly the main crop and the companions stay on the sides or front instead of filling every gap.

    • cabbage spaced as the main crop with room to head up
    • onions or chives in a lower side row
    • lettuce only as an early front-edge crop
    • dill or flowers off to the side, not woven through the center
    • space left open for airflow and easy pest checks

    What to be careful about

    Cabbage gets broad enough that even decent companions become bad ones if the patch turns into a cool damp tangle that hides leaf damage.

    • Do not crowd cabbage with more big leafy plants.
    • Do not make the heads hard to inspect.
    • Do not let old damaged leaves sit too long.
    • Do not forget that cool-season timing changes what fits nearby.

    Protect my cabbage, what actually helps?

    If cabbage leaves start looking ragged, holey, or sticky, the first troublemakers to suspect are usually cabbage worms, loopers, aphids, or flea beetles. Cabbage almost always does better when these are caught before the head gets dense and hard to inspect.

    • What to watch for Chewed leaves, green caterpillars, dark frass, sticky folds in new growth, and young plants peppered with small holes.
    • What naturally helps most Row cover, hand-picking worms, steady growth, and frequent leaf checks.
    • Check under the leaves That is where eggs and worms usually show up first.
    • Keep the bed airy Dense cabbage beds hide problems quietly.
    • Do not ignore white butterflies They are often the warning sign.
    • Clean up rough outer leaves Do not let damaged foliage just hang around the plant.

    Common cabbage troublemakers

    • Cabbage worms Chew leaves and can work down toward the head. Quick cure: hand-pick them early and keep checking.
    • Cabbage loopers Another leaf-chewing caterpillar that can build fast. Quick cure: catch them while they are still small.
    • Aphids Cluster deep in tender folds and stress the plant. Quick cure: spray them off before colonies build deep inside.
    • Flea beetles Tiny jumpy beetles that pepper young leaves with holes. Quick cure: protect small plants early and keep them vigorous.
    • Slugs Can rough up lower leaves in damp beds. Quick cure: reduce hiding spots and inspect at dusk or early morning.

    Best first move

    Turn over a few leaves and look closely before guessing, because cabbage pests usually show themselves when you actually inspect the plant.

    Good natural cabbage pest routine

    • check leaf undersides often
    • hand-pick worms early
    • use row cover when pressure is predictable
    • spray off aphids before they build
    • remove rough outer leaves promptly

    What not to do

    • Do not wait until leaves are badly chewed.
    • Do not ignore butterflies hovering around the bed.
    • Do not let cabbage stay crowded and messy.

    Natural remedies people sometimes try

    • Bt (Bacillus thuringiensis) for young cabbage worms and loopers.
    • Neem oil for early soft-bodied pest pressure, used carefully.
    • Row cover is still one of the best low-tech protections for cabbage.

    A few more cabbage basics that really matter

    • Soil pH: cabbage often does well around 6.5 to 7.5.
    • Rich soil: supports solid head growth.
    • Cool weather: usually helps more than heat.
    • Moisture: steady watering matters.
    • Spacing: important for healthier heads and cleaner leaves.

    Good next reads: Protect My Veggie, Build My Garden Plan, and the crop finder.

    Bottom line

    If you like cabbage, keep the bed practical, roomy, and easy to inspect, then add just a few helpers instead of building one giant brassica block.
