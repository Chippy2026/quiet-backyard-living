Take the $5 Herb Challenge

Most herbs do not look like much when you buy them.

A $4 basil plant. A $5 mint plant. A small pot of thyme.

They fit in one hand.

A few months later, many of those same herbs can produce far more food, seasoning, tea, starter plants, or dried herbs than most people expect.

This is not a business plan. It is simply a look at how much value a small herb purchase can realistically create in an ordinary backyard.

The ground rules

For potted herbs

  • herb plant: about $5
  • pot: about $5
  • potting mix: about $3
  • water or fertilizer share: about $2

Total startup: about $15

For in-ground herbs

  • starter plant: about $5
  • soil or compost share: about $3
  • water or fertilizer share: about $1 to $2

Total startup: about $8 to $10

The point is not perfect accounting. The point is to see what one small herb plant can become over a real season.

Step 1, pick your herb

🥇 Basil

Fast growth, easy to measure, easy to use, and one of the easiest herbs to turn into visible value fast.

🥈 Mint

Big repeat harvests, great for tea and bundles, and one of the fastest ways to see the challenge working.

🥉 Oregano

Dries beautifully and makes the value side of the challenge very easy to understand.

Bonus herbs

Thyme, chives, and parsley all work too if you want a quieter, steadier version of the challenge.

Step 2, record your startup cost

Challenge starts now.

Step 3, track every harvest

Harvest #1

4 oz basil

Store equivalent: about $4

Running total: $4

Harvest #2

6 oz basil

Store equivalent: about $6

Running total: $10

This is where the challenge starts getting fun. Once you track the first couple of harvests, you stop seeing the plant as a tiny purchase and start seeing it as a little value machine.

Step 4, see if the challenge can make a little money too

Friends and family bunches

If your herb is still producing after you have used what you want, baggie or bunch up a little extra and see if a few people around you want it.

Example: 4 basil baggies × $3 = $12

New running total: $22

Rooted starts or little bundles

If basil, mint, oregano, or chives are getting ahead of you, the extra may be more valuable as starts, tea bundles, or giftable herb bunches than as one more harvest for yourself.

Example: 6 rooted basil starts × $3 = $18

Or: 5 mint bundles × $4 = $20

The point is not to force a sale. It is to see whether one small herb challenge can first pay for itself, then maybe create a little real-world extra value beyond your own kitchen.

Value does not have to mean money

Maybe your basil becomes pesto, freezer meals, gifts for neighbors, or rooted starts for friends. That still counts.

The goal is not becoming an herb entrepreneur. The goal is seeing how much value one small plant can create when you actually track it.
