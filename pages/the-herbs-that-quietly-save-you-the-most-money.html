The Herbs That Quietly Save You the Most Money

If you want a backyard crop that can help the garden, help the kitchen, and maybe even become something extra later, herbs are hard to beat. They fit small spaces, do more than one job, and keep paying you back in quiet little ways all season long.

Some garden crops feel exciting for one weekend and then vanish into a single meal. Herbs are different. Basil, parsley, oregano, thyme, chives, and mint can keep helping in the garden, in the kitchen, and sometimes a little beyond that.

That is why herbs punch above their weight. They fit small spaces, they usually get used, and a little extra can turn into drying, sharing, dividing, or selling later.

The short answer

Best herbs to start with

  • basil for fast payoff
  • parsley for everyday use
  • oregano and thyme for drying
  • chives for easy repeat harvests
  • mint for tea, in a pot

My six best-value herb picks

Fastest payoff

1. Basil

Around $3 seed packet, around $3 fresh-bunch value

Basil gives fast obvious payoff, especially if you already cook with tomatoes, pasta, pizza, or summer sandwiches.

  • great fresh-use herb
  • easy to grow extra of
  • works well near tomatoes and peppers
Read the basil guide
Best quiet workhorse

2. Parsley

Around $3 seed packet, around $3 fresh-bunch value

Parsley is one of the easiest herbs to actually use often, which is a big part of its value.

  • good for soups, eggs, potatoes, and salads
  • easy to tuck into a productive bed
  • strong everyday herb
Best drying herb

3. Oregano

Around $3 seed packet, around $3 fresh, around $6 dried value

Oregano keeps paying you back after harvest day because it dries well and stores easily.

  • strong drying value
  • good repeat use herb
  • worth growing a little extra
Best small-but-mighty herb

4. Thyme

Around $3 seed packet, around $3 fresh, around $6 dried value

Thyme is small, durable, and useful enough to earn its place without asking for much room.

  • great for small beds and edges
  • dries well
  • easy plant to keep around long term
Read the thyme guide
Best easy snipping herb

5. Chives

Around $4 to $6 starter plant, then repeat harvests

Chives are easy to snip, easy to use, and easy to fit into little empty spaces.

  • repeat harvests
  • nice edging herb
  • low-drama and beginner-friendly
Best tea herb

6. Mint

Around $4 to $6 starter plant, lots of repeat harvest value

Mint can give a lot back fast, especially for tea and summer drinks, if you keep it contained.

  • best kept in a pot
  • great for tea
  • heavy repeat-harvest value

If I wanted the simplest starter herb setup

  • one pot of basil
  • one pot or patch of parsley
  • one thyme plant
  • one oregano plant
  • one clump of chives

That setup covers fast fresh use, drying herbs, repeat snipping, and real kitchen value without turning herbs into a whole separate project.

Keep it simple

  • grow the herbs you actually use
  • start with 3 to 5, not 15
  • mint usually belongs in a pot
  • grow a little extra if you want more value later

Bottom line

If you want one of the warmest, easiest, most practical ways for a backyard to give something back, start with herbs. They can help the garden, help the kitchen, and sometimes turn into a little extra value beyond that. A few herbs earning their space twice is one of the smartest small-space moves you can make.
