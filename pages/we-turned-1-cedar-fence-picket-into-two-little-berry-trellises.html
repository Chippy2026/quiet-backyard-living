We Turned 1 Cedar Fence Picket Into Two Little Berry Trellises

A fun little backyard project for a blackberry bush, a raspberry bush, and a cedar picket that turned out cuter than expected.

Sometimes the best backyard projects are not the big expensive ones. They are the little experiments that start with, “I wonder if this would work,” and end with something useful that also makes the yard feel more alive.

That is basically what happened here. We made two little fan trellises from one cedar fence picket, one for a blackberry bush and one for a raspberry bush, and the whole thing just looked fun from start to finish.

It was simple, cheap, a little playful, and a nice way to give the berry canes something better to lean on without buying a big fancy trellis.

The basic idea: one cedar fence picket got ripped longways into mostly 7/8 inch strips, then those strips became two simple little fan trellises that could be attached to garden fence posts for stronger support.

What we used

Main material

  • 1 cedar fence picket
  • ripped longways into narrow strips

Fasteners and assembly

  • wood glue
  • drill
  • screws
  • pin nails

How we cut it

Main fan strips

Most of the picket was ripped into about 7/8 inch strips. Five of those strips came out at that width, and one leftover strip ended up closer to about 1/2 inch.

Spreader pieces

The leftover strip was cut into 1 foot, 2 foot, and 3 foot sections so the fan could spread outward and hold its shape.

How the little fan trellises came together

Base first

We glued the fan base together first, then drilled and screwed it about 10 inches up from the bottom. That gave the trellis a stable base while still leaving a straight lower section to attach later.

Then spread the fan

After that, we glued and pin-nailed the 1 foot, 2 foot, and 3 foot sections across the fan to hold the shape open and give the berry canes more places to land.

Final step: once everything dried, we attached each trellis to a garden fence post so it had more solid support than it would have on its own.

Why we liked this project

It felt fun

This did not feel like a heavy formal build. It felt more like making something simple and useful out of what we had.

It was cheap

One cedar picket turning into two little trellises is a pretty satisfying backyard math problem.

It looks better than a generic support

The fan shape has a little charm to it, especially once it is standing there ready for the berry canes to fill in.

It fits the yard

It feels homemade in a good way, not overbuilt, not fussy, just useful and kind of cheerful.

A good fit for blackberry and raspberry

These little trellises are not trying to be giant commercial berry supports. They are just a nice way to guide a smaller blackberry or raspberry plant, give it something to grow into, and make that little part of the yard feel more intentional.

One went with the blackberry bush. One went with the raspberry bush. And both of them looked like they belonged there.
