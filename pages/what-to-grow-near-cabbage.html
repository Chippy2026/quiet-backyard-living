What to Grow Near Cabbage

Cabbage usually likes a few steady helpers nearby, especially if you want the bed to feel more balanced than one big target for pests.

Good nearby friends for cabbage

  • Dill A classic companion for cabbage-family crops.
  • Thyme Compact herb option.
  • Onions Strong-smelling and easy to mix in.
  • Garlic Another useful edge crop.
  • Nasturtiums Helpful flower support in a mixed bed.

Did you know?

  • Cabbage often benefits from the same mixed-bed logic as other brassicas.
  • Spacing and airflow matter more than people think.
  • A little diversity can make the bed feel more resilient.

A simple cabbage bed example

A simple beginner-friendly mix could look like this:

  • cabbage as the main crop
  • dill nearby
  • garlic or onions around edges
  • nasturtiums nearby

What to be careful about

Do not cram cabbage into an overcrowded patch.

  • Watch for airflow issues and pest buildup.
  • Give heads enough room to form well.

Protect my cabbage, what actually helps?

If cabbage leaves start looking ragged, holey, or sticky, the first troublemakers to suspect are usually cabbage worms, loopers, aphids, or flea beetles. Cabbage almost always does better when these are caught before the head is fully forming.

  • What to watch for Chewed leaves, green caterpillars, dark frass, sticky growth tucked in the folds, and young plants peppered with tiny holes.
  • What naturally helps most Row cover, hand-picking worms, steady growth, and frequent leaf checks.
  • Check under the leaves That is where eggs and worms usually show up first.
  • Keep the bed airy Cabbage holds trouble quietly when it gets too crowded.
  • Do not ignore white butterflies They are often a warning sign, not just background scenery.
  • Clean up rough outer leaves Do not let damaged foliage sit too long around the plant.

Common cabbage troublemakers

  • Cabbage worms Chew leaves and can work into the head area. Quick cure: hand-pick them early and keep checking.
  • Cabbage loopers Another leaf-chewing caterpillar that can build fast. Quick cure: catch them while they are still small.
  • Aphids Cluster in tender folds and stress the plant. Quick cure: spray them off before colonies build deep inside the leaves.
  • Flea beetles Tiny jumpy beetles that pepper young leaves with holes. Quick cure: protect small plants early and keep them vigorous.
  • Slugs Can rough up lower leaves in damp beds. Quick cure: reduce hiding spots and inspect at dusk or early morning.

Best first move

Turn over a few leaves and look closely before guessing, because cabbage pests usually show themselves if you actually inspect the plant.

Good natural cabbage pest routine

  • check leaf undersides often
  • hand-pick worms early
  • use row cover when pressure is predictable
  • spray off aphids before they build
  • remove rough outer leaves promptly

What not to do

  • Do not wait until leaves are badly chewed.
  • Do not ignore butterflies hovering around the bed.
  • Do not let cabbage stay crowded and messy.

Natural remedies people sometimes try

  • Bt (Bacillus thuringiensis) for young cabbage worms and loopers.
  • Neem oil for early soft-bodied pest pressure, used carefully.
  • Row cover is still one of the best low-tech protections for cabbage.

A few more cabbage basics that really matter

  • Soil pH: cabbage often does well around 6.5 to 7.5
  • Rich soil: supports head growth
  • Cool weather: usually helps more than heat
  • Moisture: steady watering matters
  • Spacing: important for healthier heads

Bottom line

If you like cabbage, keep the bed practical, give the crop what it actually needs, and add just a couple helpful neighbors instead of chasing a perfect chart.
