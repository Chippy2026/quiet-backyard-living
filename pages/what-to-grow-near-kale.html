What to Grow Near Kale

Kale usually does better with a few smart neighbors around it, especially ones that do not crowd it too hard and can help around common brassica problems. It is tougher than lettuce, but it still likes a bed that stays practical, inspectable, and not overloaded.

Kale companions at a glance

Best easy companions

Onions, dill, beets, lettuce, and flowers nearby are some of the easiest useful companions for kale.

What helps most

Airflow, clean spacing, and quick pest checks usually matter more than a long list of supposed miracle pairings.

Good use of the edges

Use the edges for onions, herbs, lettuce, or flowers, not for another bulky brassica crowding the same space.

What to avoid

Do not let the bed turn into a dense brassica patch where worms, aphids, and damp leaf messes are easy to miss.

Best kale support crew

  • Onions or scallions for lower compact company
  • Dill for a little life nearby without becoming the whole bed
  • Lettuce as a gentler front-edge crop in cooler timing
  • Beets for another useful crop that usually stays manageable
  • Flowers nearby where the bed benefits from more insect activity

Why these kale pairings actually make sense

  • Onions and scallions fit because they stay narrow. They add another edible without making the kale patch too thick to inspect.
  • Dill can work because it adds a different structure. It is not a cure-all, but it makes more sense than packing kale with even more heavy leafy crops.
  • Lettuce works best as a temporary cooler-season companion. It can use front-edge space before kale gets large.
  • Beets are often easier neighbors than another big brassica. They help keep the bed mixed without doubling down on the exact same pest problems.
  • Flowers help most on the outside of the bed. Kale benefits from a livelier edge more than from a chaotic crowded center.

What usually goes wrong

  • too many brassicas packed together
  • not checking under leaves often enough
  • letting the bed stay damp and crowded
  • treating kale like it is too tough to need regular attention

A simple kale bed layout idea

A beginner-friendly kale bed usually works best when the kale has real room to leaf out and the helper crops stay around the edges instead of filling every gap.

  • kale spaced as the main center or back crop
  • onions or scallions in a lower edge row
  • lettuce only where it will not be smothered later
  • dill or flowers off to the side, not jammed through the middle
  • space left for airflow and easy leaf inspection

What to be careful about

Kale can look sturdy enough to overpack, but brassica beds get messy fast when the leaves overlap, the air stays still, and pests settle in underneath.

  • Do not make it hard to look under the leaves.
  • Do not crowd kale with more big leafy neighbors.
  • Do not forget that cool-season timing changes what fits nearby.
  • Do not let outer leaves stay ragged and messy too long.

Protect my kale, what actually helps?

If kale leaves start looking lacey, holey, or sticky, the first troublemakers to suspect are usually cabbage worms, aphids, flea beetles, or slugs in damp beds. Kale is tougher than lettuce, but pests can still build fast if you stop checking.

  • What to watch for Chewed leaves, green caterpillars, dark frass, aphids tucked in the leaf folds, and tiny shot-hole damage on young plants.
  • What naturally helps most Row cover, hand-picking worms, steady growth, and checking leaf undersides often.
  • Check under the leaves That is where eggs, worms, and aphids often show up first.
  • Keep the bed airy Kale handles a lot, but crowded damp growth still invites trouble.
  • Do not ignore butterfly traffic White butterflies near kale are often a warning sign.
  • Harvest rough outer leaves That helps keep the plant cleaner and easier to inspect.

Common kale troublemakers

  • Cabbage worms Chew leaves fast and leave frass behind. Quick cure: hand-pick them early and keep checking.
  • Aphids Hide in folds and on tender growth. Quick cure: spray them off before they build into colonies.
  • Flea beetles Pepper young leaves with tiny holes. Quick cure: protect young plants early and keep them vigorous.
  • Slugs Can rough up leaves in cool damp weather. Quick cure: reduce hiding spots and check at dusk or early morning.
  • Cabbage loopers Another leaf-chewing caterpillar that can build quietly. Quick cure: catch them while they are still small.

Best first move

Flip a few leaves over before guessing, because kale pests usually tell on themselves if you actually look underneath.

Good natural kale pest routine

  • check leaf undersides often
  • hand-pick worms early
  • spray off aphids before they build
  • harvest rough outer leaves
  • keep the bed airy and steady

What not to do

  • Do not wait until the leaves are heavily chewed.
  • Do not ignore white butterflies around the bed.
  • Do not let kale stay crowded and messy.

Natural remedies people sometimes try

  • Bt (Bacillus thuringiensis) for young cabbage worms and loopers.
  • Neem oil for early soft-bodied pest pressure, used carefully.
  • Row cover is still one of the most useful low-tech protections for kale.

A few more kale basics that really matter

  • Soil pH: kale often does well around 6.0 to 7.5.
  • Sun: full sun to light shade can work.
  • Moisture: steady moisture helps keep growth tender.
  • Rich soil: brassicas usually appreciate decent fertility.
  • Cool weather: kale often shines in cooler conditions.
  • Airflow: helps reduce trouble in dense beds.

Bottom line

If you like kale, keep the bed inspectable, airy, and not overloaded with more big leafy crops than it can handle.
