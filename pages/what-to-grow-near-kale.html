What to Grow Near Kale

Kale usually does better with a few smart neighbors around it, especially ones that do not crowd it too hard and can help around common brassica problems.

Good nearby friends for kale

  • Dill Helpful for beneficial insects and classic brassica companion energy.
  • Onions A simple strong-smelling neighbor that can fit nearby.
  • Garlic Another useful allium-type helper to tuck around edges.
  • Nasturtiums Adds pollinator appeal and old-school distraction-crop value.
  • Marigolds Good for diversity, color, and mixed-bed feel.
  • Beets A practical edible that can often live nearby without becoming a bully.

Did you know?

  • Kale can look sturdy, but pests often find it faster than people expect.
  • Dill is one of those plants that keeps showing up in old-school brassica advice for a reason.
  • Not all leafy greens want the exact same neighbors or timing.

A simple kale bed example

A simple beginner-friendly mix could look like this:

  • kale as the main crop
  • dill nearby
  • nasturtiums or marigolds at edges
  • onions or garlic spaced around the bed

What to be careful about

Kale still needs airflow and room, even if it feels tougher than softer greens.

  • Do not crowd it with too many leafy neighbors.
  • Watch for pest buildup early.
  • Give it sun and decent spacing.

Protect my kale, what actually helps?

If kale leaves start looking lacey, holey, or sticky, the first troublemakers to suspect are usually cabbage worms, aphids, flea beetles, or slugs in damp beds. Kale is tougher than lettuce, but pests can still build fast if you stop checking.

  • What to watch for Chewed leaves, green caterpillars, dark frass, aphids tucked in the leaf folds, and tiny shot-hole damage on young plants.
  • What naturally helps most Row cover, hand-picking worms, steady growth, and checking leaf undersides often.
  • Check under the leaves That is where eggs, worms, and aphids often show up first.
  • Keep the bed airy Kale handles a lot, but crowded damp growth still invites trouble.
  • Do not ignore butterfly traffic White butterflies near kale are often a warning sign.
  • Harvest rough outer leaves That helps keep the plant cleaner and easier to inspect.

Common kale troublemakers

  • Cabbage worms Chew leaves fast and leave frass behind. Quick cure: hand-pick them early and keep checking.
  • Aphids Hide in folds and on tender growth. Quick cure: spray them off before they build into colonies.
  • Flea beetles Pepper young leaves with tiny holes. Quick cure: protect young plants early and keep them vigorous.
  • Slugs Can rough up leaves in cool damp weather. Quick cure: reduce hiding spots and check at dusk or early morning.
  • Cabbage loopers Another leaf-chewing caterpillar that can build quietly. Quick cure: catch them while they are still small.

Best first move

Flip a few leaves over before guessing, because kale pests usually tell on themselves if you actually look underneath.

Good natural kale pest routine

  • check leaf undersides often
  • hand-pick worms early
  • spray off aphids before they build
  • harvest rough outer leaves
  • keep the bed airy and steady

What not to do

  • Do not wait until the leaves are heavily chewed.
  • Do not ignore white butterflies around the bed.
  • Do not let kale stay crowded and messy.

Natural remedies people sometimes try

  • Bt (Bacillus thuringiensis) for young cabbage worms and loopers.
  • Neem oil for early soft-bodied pest pressure, used carefully.
  • Row cover is still one of the most useful low-tech protections for kale.

A few more kale basics that really matter

  • Soil pH: kale often does well around 6.0 to 7.5
  • Sun: full sun to light shade can work
  • Moisture: steady moisture helps keep growth tender
  • Rich soil: brassicas usually appreciate decent fertility
  • Cool weather: kale often shines in cooler conditions
  • Airflow: helps reduce trouble in dense beds

Bottom line

If you like kale, keep the bed simple, give the plants what they really need, and add just a couple helpful neighbors instead of trying to force a perfect companion chart.
