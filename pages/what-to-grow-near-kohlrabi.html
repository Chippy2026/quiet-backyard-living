What to Grow Near Kohlrabi

Kohlrabi fits the cooler brassica lane, but it usually feels easier when the bed is open, balanced, and not too crowded.

Kohlrabi companions at a glance

Best easy companions

Basil, chives, onions, parsley, and marigolds are some of the easiest useful neighbors for tomatoes.

What helps most

Airflow, spacing, support, and a few smaller helpers usually matter more than trying to build a perfect companion-planting chart.

Good use of the edges

Use the edges for herbs, onions, chives, or flowers, not for another big thirsty main crop.

What to avoid

Do not let the bed turn into a crowded tomato jungle where airflow disappears and every support plant becomes a competitor.

Best kohlrabi support crew

  • Basil for easy herb value nearby
  • Marigolds for flowers and edge life
  • Onions or chives for compact lower growth
  • Parsley for another useful edible
  • Lettuce only early, before kohlrabi take over

Why these kohlrabi pairings actually make sense

  • Basil works because it stays useful without taking over. It fits the same warm-season rhythm as tomatoes and gives you an herb you will probably reach for often.
  • Marigolds work best as edge flowers, not as magic pest shields. They help the bed feel alive, bring in some beneficial activity, and visually break things up.
  • Onions and chives fit because they stay lower and simpler. They can use little pockets of space without becoming a huge shading problem.
  • Parsley earns its place by being practical. It gives you another real edible without demanding the same big footprint tomatoes do.
  • Lettuce only works early. It can be a nice temporary front-edge crop, but later in the season tomatoes usually outgrow it and start winning the light-and-moisture battle.

What usually goes wrong

  • too many medium and large plants packed together
  • not enough airflow around tomato foliage
  • lettuce or other smaller crops getting buried in shade later
  • thinking “companion planting” means every inch needs a plant

A simple kohlrabi bed layout idea

A beginner-friendly kohlrabi bed often works best when the kohlrabi stay the obvious main crop and the helpers stay in supporting roles.

  • kohlrabi spaced as the main center or back crop
  • basil tucked near a few plants, not jammed everywhere
  • marigolds on edges or corners
  • chives or onions in lower side pockets
  • lettuce only as an early front-edge crop before the kohlrabi get dominant

What to be careful about

Kohlrabi need more room, airflow, sun, and moisture consistency than people sometimes expect. So even a “good companion” can become a problem if the bed gets too crowded.

  • Do not jam too many thirsty plants together.
  • Give kohlrabi room to breathe.
  • Let shorter support plants stay support plants, not full competitors.
  • Be careful with lettuce later in the season once kohlrabi get large and dominant.

Protect my kohlrabi, what actually helps?

If kohlrabi leaves start looking holey, sticky, or ragged, the first troublemakers to suspect are usually cabbage worms, aphids, flea beetles, or slugs in damp weather. Kohlrabi usually stays easiest to protect when the young plants are watched closely.

  • What to watch for Shot-hole damage, green caterpillars, sticky growth, slimy chewing, and swollen stems that never size up cleanly.
  • What naturally helps most Row cover, hand-picking worms, steady growth, and frequent leaf checks.
  • Check under the leaves That is where eggs, worms, and aphids often show up first.
  • Keep the bed airy Crowded kohlrabi is harder to inspect and easier to stress.
  • Do not ignore white butterflies They are often an early warning in brassica beds.
  • Clean up rough leaves Do not let the worst damaged foliage sit too long.

Common kohlrabi troublemakers

  • Cabbage worms Chew leaves fast and leave frass behind. Quick cure: hand-pick them early and keep checking.
  • Aphids Hide in tender growth and stress the plant. Quick cure: spray them off before colonies build deeply.
  • Flea beetles Pepper young leaves with tiny holes. Quick cure: protect small plants early and keep them vigorous.
  • Slugs Can rough up leaves in cool damp weather. Quick cure: reduce hiding spots and check at dusk or early morning.
  • Cabbage loopers Another leaf-chewing caterpillar that can build quietly. Quick cure: catch them while they are still small.

Best first move

Check the leaves and the young swollen stems early, because kohlrabi problems usually show up in the foliage before the bulb tells the story.

Good natural kohlrabi pest routine

  • check leaf undersides often
  • hand-pick worms early
  • spray off aphids before they build
  • protect young plants early
  • keep the bed airy and steady

What not to do

  • Do not ignore early shot-hole damage.
  • Do not wait until leaves are heavily chewed.
  • Do not let kohlrabi stay crowded and messy.

Natural remedies people sometimes try

  • Bt (Bacillus thuringiensis) for young cabbage worms and loopers.
  • Neem oil for early soft-bodied pest pressure, used carefully.
  • Row cover is still one of the most useful low-tech protections for kohlrabi.

A few more kohlrabi basics that really matter

  • Soil pH: this crop usually does best when the soil is reasonably balanced for vegetables.
  • Sun: make sure the crop gets the light level it actually wants.
  • Moisture: steady moisture usually helps more than extreme swings.
  • Spacing: a lot of problems start with planting too tight.
  • Timing: some crops shine in cool weather, some want real summer heat.

Bottom line

If you like kohlrabi, keep the bed practical and give the crop what it really needs first, then use companions to support it rather than complicate it.
