What to Grow Near Mizuna

Mizuna is one of those leafy crops that usually works best in the gentler cooler-season side of the garden.

Good nearby friends for mizuna

  • Herbs nearby Smaller useful herbs often make the easiest companions.
  • Flowers at the edges A little diversity can make the bed feel more alive and balanced.
  • Gentler neighboring crops Practical neighbors usually work better than aggressive ones.

Did you know?

  • Not every crop needs a big complicated support cast to do better.
  • Sometimes the best companion move is just avoiding crowding and leaving enough room.
  • Old mixed beds were often more about balance and timing than perfection.

A simple mizuna bed example

A simple beginner-friendly mix could look like this:

  • mizuna as the main crop
  • a few smaller support plants nearby
  • flowers or herbs at edges
  • enough room for airflow and easy harvesting

What to be careful about

Even good companion ideas can backfire if the bed gets too tight or the crop’s real needs get ignored.

  • Do not overpack the bed.
  • Pay attention to space, light, and moisture.
  • Keep the neighbors supportive, not competitive.

Protect my mizuna, what actually helps?

If mizuna leaves start looking lacey, peppered, or sticky, the first troublemakers to suspect are usually flea beetles, cabbage worms, aphids, or slugs in cool damp weather. Mizuna is usually quick and forgiving, but pests can make the harvest ugly in a hurry.

  • What to watch for Tiny shot holes, green caterpillars, sticky folds, slime trails, and ragged leaves that stop looking salad-worthy fast.
  • What naturally helps most Row cover, steady growth, fast inspection, and harvesting rough leaves before damage spreads.
  • Protect the young patch early Flea beetles love tender new growth.
  • Check under the leaves Eggs, aphids, and small caterpillars often hide there first.
  • Keep the bed cool and even Heat plus stress shortens mizuna’s sweet spot.
  • Harvest often Regular picking helps you catch problems while they are still manageable.

Common mizuna troublemakers

  • Flea beetles Tiny jumping beetles that pepper leaves with holes. Quick cure: protect young plants early and keep them growing steadily.
  • Cabbage worms Chew leaves quickly and leave frass behind. Quick cure: hand-pick them early and keep checking.
  • Aphids Build in leaf folds and stress tender growth. Quick cure: spray them off before colonies deepen.
  • Slugs Can rough up low leafy growth in damp weather. Quick cure: reduce hiding spots and inspect early morning or dusk.
  • Cabbage loopers Another leaf-chewing caterpillar that can build quietly. Quick cure: catch them while they are still small.

Best first move

Look at the youngest leaves and the underside of a few stems, because mizuna usually shows its pest story there first.

Good natural mizuna pest routine

  • protect young plants early
  • check leaf undersides often
  • spray off aphids before they build
  • watch for slug damage in damp stretches
  • harvest rough leaves often

What not to do

  • Do not ignore early shot-hole damage.
  • Do not let mizuna stay crowded and heat-stressed.
  • Do not wait too long on caterpillar damage.

Natural remedies people sometimes try

  • Bt (Bacillus thuringiensis) for young cabbage worms and loopers.
  • Insecticidal soap for aphids if sprayed directly.
  • Row cover is one of the best low-tech protections for tender mizuna.

A few more mizuna basics that really matter

  • Soil pH: this crop usually does best when the soil is reasonably balanced for vegetables.
  • Sun: make sure the crop gets the light level it actually wants.
  • Moisture: steady moisture usually helps more than extreme swings.
  • Spacing: a lot of problems start with planting too tight.
  • Timing: some crops shine in cool weather, some want real summer heat.

Bottom line

If you like mizuna, keep the bed practical and give the crop what it really needs first, then use companions to support it rather than complicate it.
