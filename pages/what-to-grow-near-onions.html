What to Grow Near Onions

Onions are one of those quiet useful crops that often fit best as part of the support crew around other vegetables, but they can also hold their own in a simple mixed bed.

Good nearby friends for onions

  • Herbs nearby Small useful herbs often make the easiest companions in mixed beds.
  • Flowers at edges A little diversity can help the bed feel more alive and useful.
  • Gentler neighboring crops The calmer the nearby plants, the easier the bed usually is to manage.
  • Edge crops with room Smaller practical crops often make more sense than crowding in major competitors.

Did you know?

  • Not every vegetable needs a big complicated companion plan.
  • Sometimes the best nearby plants are just the ones that do not crowd, shade, or dry out the main crop too hard.
  • Old-school mixed beds were often more about balance than perfection.

A simple onions bed example

A simple beginner-friendly mix could look like this:

  • onions as the main crop
  • a couple smaller support plants nearby
  • flowers or herbs at the edges
  • enough open room so the bed can still breathe

What to be careful about

Even a good companion idea can become a problem if the bed gets too crowded or the crop’s real needs get ignored.

  • Do not overpack the bed.
  • Pay attention to light, room, and moisture.
  • Keep support plants supportive, not competitive.

Protect my onions, what actually helps?

If onion leaves start silvering, twisting, or collapsing too early, the first problems to suspect are usually thrips, onion maggots, or stress from cramped wet conditions. Onions usually do best when trouble is caught early, before the bulbs stall out.

  • What to watch for Silvery streaks, curled leaves, stunted bulbs, soft spots near the base, and plants that weaken for no obvious reason.
  • What naturally helps most Good airflow, steady but not soggy moisture, rotation, and early checks on leaf damage.
  • Check the leaves closely Thrips damage can look subtle at first, but it adds up fast.
  • Avoid soggy crowded beds Onion problems get worse when the patch stays wet and cramped.
  • Rotate where possible Repeating onions in the same troubled spot can make maggot pressure harder to shake.
  • Clean up after harvest Do not leave onion debris sitting around if the bed had problems.

Common onion troublemakers

  • Thrips Tiny rasping pests that leave silvery streaking on leaves. Quick cure: watch leaves early and keep plants from getting dry-stressed and neglected.
  • Onion maggots Larvae that damage the base and bulbs. Quick cure: rotate crops and avoid repeating onions in the same problem area.
  • Aphids Sometimes cluster on tender growth. Quick cure: rinse them off while pressure is still light.
  • Cutworms Can clip young onions in some beds. Quick cure: inspect near the soil line and protect young plants if needed.
  • Leaf miners Sometimes leave pale winding trails in leaves. Quick cure: remove badly affected foliage early and keep the bed clean.

Best first move

Take a close look at the leaves before the bulbs stall, because early thrips damage is easy to miss from a distance.

Good natural onion pest routine

  • check leaves for silvery streaking
  • keep the bed aired out
  • avoid soggy stressed conditions
  • rotate when onion problems repeat
  • clean up spent plants after harvest

What not to do

  • Do not ignore subtle leaf damage early.
  • Do not keep onions in a wet crowded patch.
  • Do not repeat onions in the same trouble spot without a break.

Natural remedies people sometimes try

  • Insecticidal soap for thrips or aphids when sprayed where the pests actually are.
  • Neem oil for lighter early soft-bodied pest pressure, used carefully.
  • Row cover early in the season where onion maggot pressure is a known pattern.

A few more onions basics that really matter

  • Soil pH: this crop usually does best when soil is reasonably balanced for vegetables.
  • Sun: pay attention to whether it wants cooler conditions or real summer heat.
  • Moisture: steady watering usually helps more than big swings.
  • Spacing: a lot of garden problems start when crops are planted too tight.
  • Timing: some crops shine early, some later, and some want the long season.

Bottom line

If you like onions, keep the bed simple, leave enough room, and use nearby plants to support it instead of complicating it.
