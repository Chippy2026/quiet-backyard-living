# Cedar Fence Picket Birdhouse Workshop — Draft Tutorial

## Project concept
This project turns one cedar fence picket from Home Depot into a fun birdhouse build-and-decorate activity for friends and family.

It works well because:
- the base structure is simple
- the finished look has personality
- people can decorate each birdhouse differently
- the roof can remain removable for clean-out

---

## What this project is
A beginner-friendly birdhouse build using:
- one cedar fence picket
- small cut stick pieces
- front and back gable pieces
- two roof pieces
- glue and/or pin nails
- paint and decorations after assembly

This is more than a craft. It can become a real usable birdhouse if built cleanly and kept serviceable.

---

## Main project idea
The build uses a **log cabin style assembly**.

That means the cut stick pieces are layered row by row around the base, overlapping every other row to create the birdhouse body.

After the body is built:
- the gable pieces are attached
- the roof pieces are assembled
- the roof is placed on top
- the roof is **not glued down** so the birdhouse can still be cleaned out later

## Cut notes (working draft)
These notes should stay paired with the reference photos for full accuracy, but the draft cut list looks like this:

### 1/2 inch stick pieces
- Cut 2 pieces at 6 1/2 inches each
- Then cut those down into 14 pieces at 1/2 inch sticks

### 1/4 inch stick pieces
- Cut 2 pieces at 4 3/8 inches
- Cut those into 1/4 inch sticks
- Note says 18 total pieces are needed

### Gables
- Cut 2 gables
- 3 3/4 inches long each
- Cut a 45 degree peak
- Leave 1/2 inch on the bottom

### Roof pieces
- Cut 1 roof panel at 8 inches
- Cut a 2nd roof panel at 8 inches
- Cut width at 6 1/2 inches

### Base
- Cut base at 8 inches

These are still working notes and should be checked against the saved cut-reference photos before becoming final public instructions.

## Ordered assembly steps

### Step 1 — Cut the base and major parts
Start by cutting the base, roof pieces, gables, and the smaller stick pieces from the cedar fence picket.

Reference photos show:
- full cedar fence picket
- base board cut
- roof panel cuts
- gable cut reference
- cut layout reference

### Step 2 — Lay out the stick pieces on the dogear base
Place the stick pieces on the dogear base to establish the first layer of the house body.

Instruction note:
- layout stick pieces on dogear base
- glue or pin nail in place

### Step 3 — Build the body in log cabin fashion
Continue building the body one row at a time.

Important instruction notes:
- keep assembling sticks in a log cabin fashion
- one row at a time
- overlap every other row
- glue or pin nail each row

### Step 4 — Build to a total of 7 rows
The handwritten assembly note says there should be:
- **a total of 7 rows**

This gives the birdhouse body its full height before the gable and roof go on.

### Step 5 — Make the gable filler pieces
Glue or pin nail:
- 2 sets of 2 small sticks together

These become the two gable end filler pieces.

### Step 6 — Attach front, back, and filler gable pieces
Next, attach:
- front gable piece
- back gable piece
- filler gable pieces

These close the top ends of the structure and create the roof pitch.

### Step 7 — Assemble the roof
Glue and pin nail the 2 roof pieces together.

The roof assembly photos suggest this is done as a separate mini-assembly before final placement.

### Step 8 — Place the roof last
Final note from the instructions:
- finally, place the roof
- **do not glue the roof on**

Why:
- the birdhouse needs a way to be cleaned out later
- rope and eyelets can be added if desired

That is one of the smartest functional notes in the whole build.
