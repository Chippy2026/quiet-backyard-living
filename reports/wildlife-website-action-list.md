# Quiet Backyard Living — Action List

A simpler action list based on the wildlife website benchmark.

---

## Main direction
Build Quiet Backyard Living into a site that feels:
- useful
- visual
- peaceful
- practical
- seasonal
- real

This site should blend:
- backyard wildlife
- garden life
- peaceful outdoor living
- useful backyard projects

---

## Top priorities

### 1. Keep building strong how-to and project pages
This is one of the most important long-term growth engines.

Focus on pages like:
- birdhouse projects
- bird bath setup
- feeder ideas
- pollinator flower beds
- wildlife-friendly yard tips
- seasonal checklists

### 2. Use the homepage to feature strong project hooks
Keep the homepage focused on things people actually want to click.

Example:
- the cedar fence picket birdhouse teaser

### 3. Keep making the site more visual
People in this space respond to photos.

Keep improving:
- gallery page
- project pages with photos
- wildlife image sections
- seasonal imagery

### 4. Lean into seasonal content
Seasonal relevance is a major strength in this niche.

Examples:
- spring birdhouse season
- hummingbird return timing
- pollinator planting season
- fall cleanup for habitat
- winter feeder support

### 5. Keep the tone warm and practical
Do not get too corporate or too stiff.

The site should feel:
- trustworthy
- calm
- useful
- friendly
- grounded in real experience

---

## What to build next

### Website content
- more project pages
- more seasonal guides
- stronger internal links between pages
- featured project sections on the homepage

### Visual content
- keep adding real wildlife and garden photos
- use project photos more heavily
- keep building photo-based pages and gallery sections

### Lead capture
- keep improving the email signup flow
- use strong specific lead hooks
- make project guides and checklists pull their own subscribers

### Traffic
- keep Pinterest moving steadily
- keep Facebook active but not spammy
- build more content that can attract search traffic over time

---

## What to avoid
- trying to look like a giant conservation organization
- becoming too generic
- overloading the site with too many categories too early
- relying on stock-photo feel
- trying to monetize too aggressively too early

---

## Best strategic lane
Quiet Backyard Living should become:

**A visually rich, useful, seasonal backyard lifestyle and wildlife site that helps people create a more peaceful, wildlife-friendly outdoor space.**

---

## Easy next moves
If you want a simple practical checklist, do these next:

1. polish the birdhouse project into a stronger finished guide
2. keep adding visual/project content to the homepage
3. keep organizing new wildlife and garden photos for the site
4. build one more strong seasonal guide
5. keep watching analytics for what pages people actually visit

---

## Short version
The winning formula is:
- useful pages
- strong photos
- seasonal relevance
- practical projects
- warm voice
- steady traffic building

That is the path Quiet Backyard Living should stay on.
