What to Grow Near Strawberries

Strawberries usually do best with neighbors that stay fairly gentle, useful, and not too competitive around their shallow root zone.

Strawberries companions at a glance

Best easy companions

Basil, chives, onions, parsley, and marigolds are some of the easiest useful nearby support plants if they fit the crop and the season.

What helps most

Airflow, spacing, support, and a few smaller helpers usually matter more than trying to build a perfect companion-planting chart.

Good use of the edges

Use the edges for herbs, onions, chives, or flowers, not for another big thirsty main crop.

What to avoid

Do not let the bed turn into a crowded plant tangle where airflow disappears and every support plant becomes a competitor.

Best strawberries support crew

  • Basil for easy herb value nearby
  • Marigolds for flowers and edge life
  • Onions or chives for compact lower growth
  • Parsley for another useful edible
  • Lettuce only early, before strawberries take over

Why these strawberries pairings actually make sense

  • Basil works because it stays useful without taking over. It fits the same general growing rhythm as the crop and gives you an herb you will probably reach for often.
  • Marigolds work best as edge flowers, not as magic pest shields. They help the bed feel alive, bring in some beneficial activity, and visually break things up.
  • Onions and chives fit because they stay lower and simpler. They can use little pockets of space without becoming a huge shading problem.
  • Parsley earns its place by being practical. It gives you another real edible without demanding the same same big main-crop footprint.
  • Lettuce only works early. It can be a nice temporary front-edge crop, but later in the season the main crop usually outgrows it and starts winning the light-and-moisture battle.

What usually goes wrong

  • too many medium and large plants packed together
  • not enough airflow around the main crop
  • lettuce or other smaller crops getting buried in shade later
  • thinking “companion planting” means every inch needs a plant

A simple strawberries bed layout idea

A beginner-friendly strawberries bed often works best when the strawberries stay the obvious main crop and the helpers stay in supporting roles.

  • strawberries spaced as the main center or back crop
  • basil tucked near a few plants, not jammed everywhere
  • marigolds on edges or corners
  • chives or onions in lower side pockets
  • lettuce only as an early front-edge crop before the strawberries get dominant

What to be careful about

Strawberries need more room, airflow, sun, and moisture consistency than people sometimes expect. So even a “good companion” can become a problem if the bed gets too crowded.

  • Do not jam too many thirsty plants together.
  • Give strawberries room to breathe.
  • Let shorter support plants stay support plants, not full competitors.
  • Be careful with lettuce later in the season once strawberries get large and dominant.

Protect my strawberries, what actually helps?

If your berries are getting chewed, slimed, or ruined right before ripening, the first troublemakers to suspect are usually slugs, sap beetles, spittlebugs, or other pests that move in when fruit stays damp and hidden.

  • What to watch for Chewed ripe fruit, slime trails, distorted berries, foamy spittle masses, and fruit that rots or disappears low in the bed.
  • What naturally helps most Mulch that keeps fruit cleaner, better airflow, quick harvests, and regular slug checks.
  • Keep fruit off wet soil Strawberries usually stay healthier when berries are lifted off soggy ground.
  • Harvest often Overripe fruit attracts more trouble fast.
  • Thin crowded growth Dense leafy beds stay damp and harder to inspect.
  • Clean up damaged fruit Do not leave spoiled berries sitting in the patch.

Common strawberry troublemakers

  • Slugs Love ripe low fruit and damp cover. Quick cure: reduce hiding spots, water earlier, and check at dusk or early morning.
  • Sap beetles Small beetles drawn to damaged overripe fruit. Quick cure: harvest promptly and remove spoiled berries right away.
  • Spittlebugs Foamy-looking masses on stems and crowns. Quick cure: rinse or remove them early before the patch gets crowded.
  • Aphids Sometimes cluster on tender growth. Quick cure: spray them off early and keep the patch from getting overgrown.
  • Birds Not an insect, but often a real strawberry thief. Quick cure: use netting if berries keep vanishing just as they ripen.

Best first move

Lift the leaves and look low in the patch, because strawberry trouble usually starts close to the fruit, not out in the open.

Good natural strawberry pest routine

  • keep berries mulched and cleaner
  • harvest ripe fruit quickly
  • check for slugs regularly
  • thin crowded leaves when needed
  • remove damaged fruit fast

What not to do

  • Do not let ripe berries sit too long in wet cover.
  • Do not let the bed stay crowded and soggy.
  • Do not ignore spoiled fruit in the patch.

Natural remedies people sometimes try

  • Iron phosphate slug bait around the bed when slug pressure is real.
  • Beer traps as an old-school slug tool, though they are rarely enough alone.
  • Insecticidal soap for aphids or spittlebug pressure on tender growth.

A few more strawberries basics that really matter

  • Soil pH: strawberries often prefer slightly acidic soil around 5.5 to 6.8
  • Sun: full sun is usually best
  • Moisture: steady moisture matters
  • Mulch: helps keep fruit cleaner and moisture steadier
  • Airflow: helps reduce trouble around fruit
  • Renewal: beds often need refreshing over time

Bottom line

If you like strawberries, keep the bed simple, give the plants what they really need, and add just a couple helpful neighbors instead of trying to force a perfect companion chart.
