What to Grow Near Microgreens

Microgreens are less about companion planting in a big mixed bed and more about timing, tenderness, and easy harvesting, but they still fit the grow-what-you-eat mindset.

Microgreens companions at a glance

Best easy companions

Basil, chives, onions, parsley, and marigolds are some of the easiest useful nearby support plants if they fit the crop and the season.

What helps most

Airflow, spacing, support, and a few smaller helpers usually matter more than trying to build a perfect companion-planting chart.

Good use of the edges

Use the edges for herbs, onions, chives, or flowers, not for another big thirsty main crop.

What to avoid

Do not let the bed turn into a crowded plant tangle where airflow disappears and every support plant becomes a competitor.

Best microgreens support crew

  • Basil for easy herb value nearby
  • Marigolds for flowers and edge life
  • Onions or chives for compact lower growth
  • Parsley for another useful edible
  • Lettuce only early, before microgreens take over

Why these microgreens pairings actually make sense

  • Basil works because it stays useful without taking over. It fits the same general growing rhythm as the crop and gives you an herb you will probably reach for often.
  • Marigolds work best as edge flowers, not as magic pest shields. They help the bed feel alive, bring in some beneficial activity, and visually break things up.
  • Onions and chives fit because they stay lower and simpler. They can use little pockets of space without becoming a huge shading problem.
  • Parsley earns its place by being practical. It gives you another real edible without demanding the same same big main-crop footprint.
  • Lettuce only works early. It can be a nice temporary front-edge crop, but later in the season the main crop usually outgrows it and starts winning the light-and-moisture battle.

What usually goes wrong

  • too many medium and large plants packed together
  • not enough airflow around the main crop
  • lettuce or other smaller crops getting buried in shade later
  • thinking “companion planting” means every inch needs a plant

A simple microgreens bed layout idea

A beginner-friendly microgreens bed often works best when the microgreens stay the obvious main crop and the helpers stay in supporting roles.

  • microgreens spaced as the main center or back crop
  • basil tucked near a few plants, not jammed everywhere
  • marigolds on edges or corners
  • chives or onions in lower side pockets
  • lettuce only as an early front-edge crop before the microgreens get dominant

What to be careful about

Microgreens need more room, airflow, sun, and moisture consistency than people sometimes expect. So even a “good companion” can become a problem if the bed gets too crowded.

  • Do not jam too many thirsty plants together.
  • Give microgreens room to breathe.
  • Let shorter support plants stay support plants, not full competitors.
  • Be careful with lettuce later in the season once microgreens get large and dominant.

Protect my microgreens, what actually helps?

If a tray or patch of microgreens starts thinning, collapsing, or getting chewed, the first troublemakers to suspect are usually fungus gnats, damping-off conditions, slugs, or aphids depending on how and where they are being grown. Microgreens are so fast and tender that prevention matters more than rescue.

  • What to watch for Weak stems, patchy collapse, tiny flying gnats, slime damage, and trays that stay too wet too long.
  • What naturally helps most Clean growing setup, good airflow, careful watering, and cutting the crop before problems get a head start.
  • Do not keep them soggy Wet stagnant conditions are half the battle here.
  • Keep airflow up Tender dense growth needs it.
  • Start clean every time Microgreens do better with simple hygiene than complicated fixes.
  • Harvest on time Waiting too long increases risk for a crop this tender.

Common microgreens troublemakers

  • Fungus gnats Love wet growing setups and can become a nuisance fast. Quick cure: let the surface dry a bit more between waterings if appropriate.
  • Damping-off conditions Not an insect, but one of the biggest microgreen losses. Quick cure: improve airflow and avoid soggy overcrowded trays.
  • Slugs Can chew outdoor microgreens quickly. Quick cure: reduce hiding spots and inspect early morning.
  • Aphids Occasionally build on tender growth. Quick cure: start fresh if a small tray is badly infested.
  • Cutworms Can clip outdoor seedlings near the base. Quick cure: inspect if a patch suddenly collapses in spots.

Best first move

Check moisture and airflow before blaming pests, because microgreen losses are often about wet stagnant conditions first.

Good natural microgreens pest routine

  • keep the setup clean
  • avoid soggy stagnant conditions
  • maintain airflow
  • harvest on time
  • restart quickly if a small tray goes bad

What not to do

  • Do not keep microgreens wetter than they need to be.
  • Do not overcrowd trays and then ignore airflow.
  • Do not spend more time rescuing a failed tray than restarting it cleanly.

Natural remedies people sometimes try

  • Yellow sticky traps for fungus gnat monitoring around indoor setups.
  • Bottom watering carefully to keep stems drier up top.
  • Fresh clean starts are often the most honest fix.

A few more microgreens basics that really matter

  • Soil pH: this crop usually does best when the soil is reasonably balanced for vegetables.
  • Sun: make sure the crop gets the light level it actually wants.
  • Moisture: steady moisture usually helps more than extreme swings.
  • Spacing: a lot of problems start with planting too tight.
  • Timing: some crops shine in cool weather, some want real summer heat.

Bottom line

If you like microgreens, keep the bed practical and give the crop what it really needs first, then use companions to support it rather than complicate it.
