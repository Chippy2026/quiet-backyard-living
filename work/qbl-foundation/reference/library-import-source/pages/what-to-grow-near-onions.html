    What to Grow Near Onions

    Onions are one of those quiet useful crops that often work best as part of the support crew around other vegetables, but they can also hold their own in a simple mixed bed. The best companions are usually crops that do not smother them, do not keep the patch too wet, and still leave the row easy to weed and harvest.

    Onion companions at a glance

    Best easy companions

    Carrots, lettuce, beets, strawberries, and herbs nearby are some of the simplest sensible companions for onions.

    What helps most

    Spacing, clean rows, and neighbors that do not build a damp jungle usually matter more than a huge pairing list.

    Good use of the edges

    Use the edges for lettuce, herbs, or strawberries where they stay manageable, not another bulky crop that hides the row.

    What to avoid

    Do not crowd onions with plants that make the patch too wet, shady, or hard to weed and inspect.

    Best onion support crew

    • Carrots as a classic nearby companion
    • Lettuce for gentler front-edge use in cooler timing
    • Beets for another manageable root crop
    • Strawberries in some mixed beds where spacing stays clean
    • Herbs nearby for kitchen value without too much overhead bulk

    Why these onion pairings actually make sense

    • Carrots fit because they use the bed differently. They do not create the same kind of above-ground crowding as a heavy leafy crop.
    • Lettuce works best when the timing stays gentle. It makes sense early or on the edge, but not if it turns the row into a wet cluttered strip.
    • Beets are often easier neighbors than a thirsty sprawling crop. They help the bed stay productive without burying the onions.
    • Strawberries can fit in some mixed beds. The key is that the patch still has to stay weedable, dry enough, and easy to inspect.
    • Herbs belong nearby, not on top of the row. Onions like company that stays useful without taking over.

    What usually goes wrong

    • too many dense leafy crops around the bulbs
    • letting the patch stay wet and weedy
    • forgetting onions need easy harvest access too
    • building a mixed bed that is harder to manage than it is worth

    A simple onion bed layout idea

    A beginner-friendly onion bed usually works best when the onion row stays visible, weedable, and not buried under heavier companions.

    • onions in a clean row or band with open access
    • carrots or beets nearby, but not too tightly mixed
    • lettuce only where it will not keep the row too wet later
    • herbs off to the side, not smothering the bulbs
    • enough open space to weed, inspect, and harvest cleanly

    What to be careful about

    Onions are easy to underestimate. They look simple, but the row can go downhill quickly if it stays damp, crowded, or hard to manage.

    • Do not bury onions in too much foliage.
    • Do not make the patch hard to weed.
    • Do not crowd bulbs with plants that trap too much moisture.
    • Do not ignore harvest access when planning the bed.

    Protect my onions, what actually helps?

    If onion leaves start silvering, twisting, or collapsing too early, the first problems to suspect are usually thrips, onion maggots, or stress from cramped wet conditions. Onions usually do best when trouble is caught early, before the bulbs stall out.

    • What to watch for Silvery streaks, curled leaves, stunted bulbs, soft spots near the base, and plants that weaken for no obvious reason.
    • What naturally helps most Good airflow, steady but not soggy moisture, rotation, and early checks on leaf damage.
    • Check the leaves closely Thrips damage can look subtle at first, but it adds up fast.
    • Avoid soggy crowded beds Onion problems get worse when the patch stays wet and cramped.
    • Rotate where possible Repeating onions in the same troubled spot can make maggot pressure harder to shake.
    • Clean up after harvest Do not leave onion debris sitting around if the bed had problems.

    Common onion troublemakers

    • Thrips Tiny rasping pests that leave silvery streaking on leaves. Quick cure: watch leaves early and keep plants from getting dry-stressed and neglected.
    • Onion maggots Larvae that damage the base and bulbs. Quick cure: rotate crops and avoid repeating onions in the same problem area.
    • Aphids Sometimes cluster on tender growth. Quick cure: rinse them off while pressure is still light.
    • Cutworms Can clip young onions in some beds. Quick cure: inspect near the soil line and protect young plants if needed.
    • Leaf miners Sometimes leave pale winding trails in leaves. Quick cure: remove badly affected foliage early and keep the bed clean.

    Best first move

    Take a close look at the leaves before the bulbs stall, because early thrips damage is easy to miss from a distance.

    Good natural onion pest routine

    • check leaves for silvery streaking
    • keep the bed aired out
    • avoid soggy stressed conditions
    • rotate when onion problems repeat
    • clean up spent plants after harvest

    What not to do

    • Do not ignore subtle leaf damage early.
    • Do not keep onions in a wet crowded patch.
    • Do not repeat onions in the same trouble spot without a break.

    Natural remedies people sometimes try

    • Insecticidal soap for thrips or aphids when sprayed where the pests actually are.
    • Neem oil for lighter early soft-bodied pest pressure, used carefully.
    • Row cover early in the season where onion maggot pressure is a known pattern.

    A few more onion basics that really matter

    • Soil pH: onions usually do best in reasonably balanced garden soil.
    • Sun: full sun helps the bulbs size up better.
    • Moisture: steady watering helps, but soggy soil does not.
    • Spacing: crowded bulbs usually stay smaller and dirtier.
    • Clean rows: onion patches are much easier when weeds do not take over.

    Good next reads: Protect My Veggie, Build My Garden Plan, and the crop finder.

    Bottom line

    If you like onions, keep the row clean, airy, and easy to manage, then use companions that support the patch instead of cluttering it.
